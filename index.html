How it works

Plan it on the iPhone. Run it with the coach.

Three steps, and the coach talks to you from the first kilometre.

1

Plan a guided workout in IQ Plan

Pick run or walk, a goal and a pace: your own, or one the app predicts from your workout history. Go as it is, or shape the session segment by segment, or let a built-in template lay it out in one tap.

2

Send it to your Apple Watch

One tap syncs the plan to the Watch, where it waits for you. Or start it on the iPhone alone (iOS 26 or later) and the coach speaks through your headphones.

3

The coach guides you in real time

Speed up, ease off, back on track: spoken cues that follow your own drift, correcting you once you have really left your pace, not at every wobble. The screen shows where you are inside your pace window.

Before you judge it: give PaceIQ two or three guided outdoor workouts rather than one. The coach learns your own stride during your first outdoor workouts, and that is when its guidance becomes precise. A guided workout is one you plan with a target pace in the IQ Plan tab; the quick free session on the Watch records your workout but does not coach.
Strengths at a glance

What PaceIQ does well

A two-minute read. Everything here is in the shipping app.

Planning

  • Quick and flexible. Pick run or walk, a goal and a pace: your own, or one the app predicts from your own workout history.
  • Shape the session segment by segment, or let a built-in template lay it out for you: intervals, tempo, Yasso 800s, run/walk.
  • Save and share. Keep plans to reuse, and share them as a file with a friend, a group or a coach who also has Elite.

During the workout

  • A coach that talks to you in real time (speed up, ease off, back on track) and follows your own drift: it corrects you once you have really left your pace, not at every wobble.
  • An effort ceiling. Set one and the coach tells you to ease off when your heart rate goes above it, even if that means falling behind your pace target.
  • It learns your stride during your first outdoor workouts and keeps refining it. It speaks 17 languages.
  • The Watch works on its own. Start a quick free session straight from the wrist, with no pace guidance, no finish goal and no iPhone. Or, once a plan is on the Watch, start it again and again with no iPhone around.

After the workout

  • Any app's workouts, deeper with PaceIQ. PaceIQ also analyses runs and walks that other apps save to Apple Health. Workouts recorded with PaceIQ go deeper: plan versus actual for every segment, weather snapshots along the session, the Watch's own climb and descent measurement, heart-rate recovery.
  • On your own devices. PaceIQ analyses your workouts on your own devices; there is no PaceIQ server. You choose whether to share a workout or paste its briefing into an AI assistant.
  • Honest cards. The analysis cards explain how their scores are computed, and say so when the data is missing instead of inventing a number. The running-form and fluid-loss explanations link to the published research they draw on, by DOI.
  • A briefing for the AI assistant you already use. Elite copies a briefing of one workout, or of up to 30 recent workouts as a trend, to your clipboard. Paste it into any AI assistant, free or paid. PaceIQ charges no AI fee, and the briefing goes nowhere unless you paste it.

Price

$34.99 a year for Elite, the full tier (US store)

A year, not a month. Pro is $9.99 a year: full workout history, the advanced workout calculator and the detailed heart-rate graph. Elite adds the personal coach on your Watch, the physiological and running-style analysis, and the AI briefings. The free tier shows your last workout.

What it is not

No multi-week training plans and no social network. It is built for Apple devices only. An Apple Watch gives you the complete experience. An iPhone alone, on iOS 26 or later, gives you the full guided, spoken workout, with a Live Activity card on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, and a Bluetooth heart-rate strap adds the heart-rate analytics. Running-style analysis needs the Watch, and the app says so instead of showing numbers it does not have.

Loading a plan

  1. Download the file on your iPhone, or AirDrop it from a Mac.
  2. Open the file: PaceIQ shows an import preview with the plan's name and segments.
  3. Load it into IQ Plan, or save it to your plan library, then send it to the Watch.

Opening a plan file needs Elite.
